Why TP53 matters
One of the most important failure points in cancer biology
TP53 encodes p53, often called the guardian of the genome
Mutated in more than 50% of human cancers and silenced across many others
Functional TP53 restoration can trigger apoptosis, halt the cell cycle, or reestablish key checkpoints
Initial focus is TNBC, with preliminary efficacy signals observed in additional cancer types
Why now

The timing for tumor suppressor restoration has changed

Advances in cancer biology, gene therapy, and translational development are converging to make tumor suppressor restoration a viable therapeutic strategy in ways that were not previously possible.

Science

A broader cancer biology story, not just a delivery story

Indel’s thesis centers on restoring functional TP53 in cancer cells. The scientific narrative focuses on reestablishing core tumor suppressor function in cancers where those safeguards have been lost.

The TNBC problem

Triple-negative breast cancer remains a high-need setting with limited targeted treatment options and a standard of care still anchored in chemotherapy, with or without checkpoint inhibition.

The solution

Indel is developing a non-viral TP53 gene therapy designed to reintroduce functional TP53 copies into cancer cells and restore the biology that normally prevents damaged cells from dividing.

Why it matters

If functional TP53 is restored, cancer cells may undergo apoptosis, halt cell-cycle progression, or recover key checkpoint controls that suppress malignant growth.

Data & Opportunity

Early proof of concept in TNBC with broader mutation-driven relevance

The current data package supports TNBC as the lead indication while reinforcing the broader relevance of TP53 restoration across mutation-driven cancers.

Preclinical proof of concept
~75% tumor growth inhibition

In TNBC xenograft models in vivo following Indel-53 treatment, early data highlights approximately 75% tumor growth inhibition. Across seven in vitro cancer models tested to date, the five with TP53 mutations have shown impact from treatment, supporting the mutation-linked rationale behind the platform.

Development Timeline

Lead program advancing toward IND with expansion beyond TNBC

TNBC is the lead entry point, with cancer subtype expansion following and an IND objective in 2028 anchored by validation, safety, and manufacturing milestones.

Discovery
