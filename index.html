Why TP53 matters
A tumor suppressor strategy built around one of cancer biology’s central failure points
TP53 encodes p53, often called the guardian of the genome
Mutated in more than 50% of human cancers and silenced across many others
Functional TP53 restoration can trigger apoptosis, halt the cell cycle, or reestablish key checkpoints
Initial focus is TNBC, with preliminary efficacy signals observed in additional cancer types
Science

A broader cancer biology story, not just a delivery story

Indel’s thesis centers on restoring functional TP53 in cancer cells. Rather than positioning the company around delivery mechanics alone, the scientific narrative focuses on reestablishing core tumor suppressor function in cancers where those safeguards have been lost.

The TNBC problem

Triple-negative breast cancer remains a high-need setting with limited targeted treatment options and a standard of care still anchored in chemotherapy, with or without checkpoint inhibition.

The solution

Indel is developing a non-viral TP53 gene therapy designed to reintroduce functional TP53 copies into cancer cells and restore the biology that normally prevents damaged cells from dividing.

Why it matters

If functional TP53 is restored, cancer cells may undergo apoptosis, halt cell-cycle progression, or recover key checkpoint controls that suppress malignant growth.

Data & Opportunity

Early proof of concept with a large downstream opportunity

The story combines compelling early efficacy, a broad biologic rationale, and meaningful commercial expansion potential.

Preclinical proof of concept
~75% tumor growth inhibition

In TNBC xenograft models in vivo following Indel-53 treatment, early data highlights approximately 75% tumor growth inhibition, consistent response across animals, and encouraging signals in additional cancer types.

Development Timeline

Lead program advancing toward IND with expansion beyond TNBC

TNBC is the lead entry point, with cancer subtype expansion following and an IND objective in 2028 anchored by validation, safety, and manufacturing milestones.

Discovery

TP53 expression restored, in vitro TNBC efficacy established, and first in vivo efficacy demonstrated.

Preclinical

Xenograft efficacy, advanced model validation, dose and durability work, and PK/PD plus safety studies.

IND-enabling

GLP tox studies, CMC and GMP preparation, pre-IND engagement, and lead candidate selection.

Clinical path

IND submission target by 2028, followed by Phase 1 development and broader cancer subtype expansion.
